## **Temperature addition to saved hour based power consumption dataframe(df_h) and further analysis:-**

- _Although its a general notion that the power consumption tends to increase with as the outside temperture increases (given that AC is in operation for same durations at varying outside temperature), just because the compressor has to work more with increaseing temperature differnece between source and sink, our conclusions don't suggest the same.But we need to analyze the peculiarities of this particular case._
- After saving the above file, an additional column for temperature of Gurgaon was added from site:- <https://www.timeanddate.com/>.
- We had the minimum and maximum temperature of Gurgaon for each 6 hour span for a day given, fro which we extracted the mean of each of those spans. So effectively we had 4 set of mean temperatures for 6 different timespans of the day (0-6 hrs, 6-12 hrs, 12-18 hrs, 18-24 hrs).
- And so the above extracted temperature was appened to earlier saved df_h dataframe and the resultant sheet was uploaded back to drive, where the colab notebook is present.
- The above sheet with hourly data was opened in pandas as **_df_new_**, and a new dataframe **_df_day_** was constructed generating mean values of hourly power consumption data along with mean temperature over a day.
- The temperature and ACs in **_df_day_** power consumptions were scaled to 0-1 and combined plot (all ACs power consumption and temperature together) and separate plot (each of 18 ACs vs date-time and temp vs date-time) was done to get qualitative insight of correlation between ACs power and temperature. **_The line subplots qualitatively show that outside average daily temperture is not positively correlated to the average daily power consumption of each AC_**.
- Also a correlation matrix of 18 ACs and temperature data among themselves was observed over a heatmap. **_The heatmap further confirms that temperature is no positively correlated with ACs power consumption quantitatively by the correlation scores._**
- **_Although many of the AC/temperature correlation are positive with quite a lot having a score near 0.50 (AC5, AC6, AC7, AC8, AC11, AC 12. AC 14, AC17); none have significant high score (> 0.50, maybe atleast 0.7) to ascertain that there is high positive correlation between AC power consumption and outside temperature. Infact there are cases like of AC 18 where the correlation score is infact negative (-0.24). These evidence suggests that in relation with the varying usage of different ACs, it all boils to residents of rooms corresponding to AC using the ACs in varying capacities according to their personal comfort, habits and adaptiblity that may not significantly change with change in weather. For instance, the residents of room 18 (AC 18) have used that AC at maximum load at most times whereas residents of room 5 (AC 5) have used that AC at lower loads at most times._**

## **Power forecast (model development):-**

- The earlier saved **_df_new_** hourly data of ACs power consumption and temperature was considered. Initially preprocessing steps generate cos and sine cyclic curves from **date_cum_time_duration** column.
- The **day_cos** column has been generated to inculcate the cyclic nature of days (_each day has 24 hours and starts with 00 hrs_). Since there may be two points in day having same day_cos value, just to maintain to maintain uniqueness of those two timestamps, additional **day_sine** column has been generated. Also since similar times at adjacent days (lets say 1PM on 1<sup>st</sup> sept and 1PM on 2<sup>nd</sup> sept)and similar days at adjacent months (lets say 1<sup>st</sup> aug and 1<sup>st</sup> sept) might have same value of day_cos and day_sine, additional **_month_cos and month_sine_** columns have been generated to preserve uniqueness of such timeframes_. The day_cos and day_sine curves have been generated considering 24 hr cycle in a day and month_cos and month_sine have been generated considering 365.25 days cycle in a year._ The final dataframe made has been named **_df_preprocess_.**
- For prediction, LSTM based sequential artitechure has been used with 2 LSTM layers both having dropout of 0.2 and a final dense layer for output. A total of 300 epoch steps have been used with early stopping based on performance of validation loss. Also 'mean absolute error' has been considered as validation error while 'mean squared error' has been used as loss. In total there are 18 models of this type, each learning the corresponding AC power sequence. Here are the list of the hyperparameters that have been used:-
    - **Lag and n_ahead:-** Lag here means number of timesteps in prior that should be used to predict of output n_ahead** units. _For example lag=3 and n_ahead =1 in our case means, the prediction of next hour power consumption based on previous 3 hours._ Depending on the complexity of sequences different combinations of lag and n_ahead has been used while tuning those models.
    - **ac_name:-** The name of the AC whose sequence is to be learnt.
    - **min_delta:-** This is one of the early stopping parameters. It specifies a minimum value of decrement (in our case of minimization of validation loss), that can be considered as improvement.
    - **Patience:-** This is 2nd of the early stopping parameters. Often, the first sign of no further improvement may not be the best time to stop training. This is because the model may coast into a plateau of no improvement or even get slightly worse before getting much better. We can account for this by adding a delay to the trigger in terms of the number of epochs on which we would like to see no improvement. This can be done by setting the "_patience_" argument.
    - **Epoch:-** The total number of training steps. Its 300 for our case and fixed.
    - **Batch_size:-** The total number of train examples to be batched at a time inside and epoch for training. This keeps changing over the course of learning those 18 models (roughly between 40 to 150).
    - **Train_days:-** The number of days whose data is to be considered for training out of total data of 61 days (august + september). This has been kept fixed at 51 days for our case. So the test/ validation set naturally has 10 days in all the cases.
    - **lr:-** It's the learning rate for our optimizer (_adam_)**.** This keeps changing over the course of learning those 18 models (roughly between 0.001 to 0.002).
    - **hidden_output_dim :-** It's the hidden_output_dimension of the intermediate states of LSTM that one gets while traversing along the timesteps during feed foraward step of LSTM. It keeps changing over the course of learning those 18 models (roughly between 10 to 40).
    - **Feature_column:-** contains columns that need to be taken from main df_preprocess dataframe for making the training data for model of particular AC. _In most of the cases the feature column had \['date-time', ac_name, 'day_cos', 'day_sin', 'month_cos', 'month_sin'\] list values except for AC 3 and AC 6 where we had \['date-time', ac_name\] list values only as this was yielding better results._
